At the start of 2026 I launched a road cycling channel on TikTok and Instagram – with no media budget, no client and no existing audience. Every view had to be earned by the content itself.

## Why I started it

Mostly for fun – and for the freedom to try new cuts, formats and ideas independently of my job: no brief, no approval loop, no brand guideline.

That freedom came with a hard constraint. Without budget, there is no way to buy reach. If a video did not hold attention on its own, nobody saw it – which makes every post an honest test.

## Choosing the angle

Most road cycling accounts show peak performance: fast rides, expensive gear, training data. I went the other way – a beginner's perspective, small steps and honest fails. That angle is less crowded, and it is easier to relate to: most people on a bike are much closer to a beginner than to a pro.

It also meant leaving things out. The content stays personal rather than technical, because the channel only works if it feels like it is made by someone who is still learning.

## What I tested

The channel is my testing ground for openings and formats. The three strongest posts so far stand for three different hook types:

- **The relatable moment.** "Jedes Mal so" (every single time) picks up a situation riders know from their own experience – 78.7K views, the best post so far.
- **Self-irony with an aspiration.** "Kurz wie ein Profi fühlen" (feeling like a pro for a moment) plays with the gap between beginner and pro – 44.9K views.
- **The question to the community.** "Was sind eure Essentials?" (what are your essentials?) invites answers instead of only showing something – 12.5K views.

What they have in common: the viewer recognises themselves within the first seconds, before the video has explained anything.

> Without budget, the hook is the only distribution you have.

The honest reading: two posts carry about two thirds of all views. Without them, the other 25 posts average around 2'700 views each – that is the normal day. Short-form reach is not linear. A channel grows through occasional outliers, and the work is to understand why they travelled and to repeat the pattern, not to expect every post to hit.

## What it gives back to client work

Running my own channel made me stricter about the first seconds. I judge a concept by its opening before anything else, I plan formats as series that can be repeated and compared, and I read a single viral post as a hypothesis, not as a result. And because there is no budget to hide behind, every number on the channel shows what the content achieved on its own.
